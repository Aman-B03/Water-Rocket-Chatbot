Engineering Design Process 
(Design, Build, Test, Analyze, Improve, Demonstrate)


Identify the Need
Design and construct a functional, safe, and sustainable water rocket prototype that meets the requirements of the AUS Water Rocket Challenge and demonstrates application of the engineering design process. 

Define the Problem
Develop a water rocket that achieves the greatest possible horizontal flight distance while complying with competition constraints, maintaining safety, minimizing costs, and using materials efficiently.

Alternative solutions
DESIGN 1: Two 1.5L water bottles taped back to back, with 4 triangular fins 
4 triangular fins are easier to build and make it more stable but also increase drag and mass
2 bottles make more volume but also more mass
Attaching the two bottles without any leaks may be difficult 
Still water bottles are cheaper but withstand less pressure

DESIGN 2: One large 1.5L carbonated drink bottle, with a cardstock nose cone, and 3 swept-back clipped delta fins. 
Clipped delta fins take more time to construct but they balance aerodynamics and stability better. 
1 bottle instead of 2, and 3 fins instead of 4 reduce mass
Specialized pointed nose cone reduces drag 
Carbonated drink bottle is made of stronger plastic built to withstand pressure

DESIGN 3: A small 500 liter bottle with a cardboard frame
Smaller bottle has less mass but holds less water
Cardboard frames are more creative and aesthetically pleasing but may be harder to construct. 
Cardboard can be heavier and weaken when wet
The frame structure is bulky and less aerodynamic.

Each design was rated by the team for each criteria on a scale of 1-10, with 10 being the strongest. Each criteria score was multiplied by its weight and summed to get the total score for each design. 

Decide
DESIGN 2 scored the highest. It scored the highest on aerodynamics and stability, as well as mass, which are the most important factors for a far horizontal flight distance. It also balanced those factors with sustainability and ease of assembly.

Design Choices
Water Level
400 mL of water (32% of bottle capacity) provides a starting balance between expelled water and compressed-air volume.
Different water levels will be tested to determine which produces the greatest horizontal distance.
Performance
The 1.25 L Pepsi bottle provides a compact, lightweight design that may reduce drag and overall mass.
An initial 45° launch angle will be considered to achieve a long horizontal distance, subject to testing and instructor approval.
Stability
Three clipped-delta fins, spaced 120° apart near the rear, help maintain a stable flight direction.
Approximately 30 g of nose ballast may be added to shift the center of mass forward if stability testing indicates it is needed.
Aerodynamic Efficiency
The conical nose and smooth bottle body provide a streamlined shape to reduce air resistance.
Three swept fins aim to provide sufficient stability while limiting additional drag and weight.
Structural Integrity
The carbonated-drink PET bottle is designed to withstand beverage carbonation pressure and will remain intact without cuts or modifications.
Reinforced fin attachments and a tape-covered nose cone improve durability.
Launch pressure will remain within instructor-approved safety limits.
Material Efficiency
Lightweight cardstock and foam board or corrugated plastic minimize unnecessary mass and material consumption.
The three-fin configuration uses fewer components than a four-fin design while aiming to maintain adequate stability.
Sustainability
Reusing a PET beverage bottle and incorporating recycled materials reduces the need for new resources.
Replaceable external components allow repairs without rebuilding the entire rocket, provided the pressure bottle remains safe to reuse.
Creativity
The combination of a custom conical nose and clipped-delta fins creates a distinctive, aerospace-inspired design.
Precisely measured, customizable components combine functionality, visual appeal, and cost-effective construction.
